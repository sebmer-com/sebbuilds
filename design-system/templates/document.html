Abstract

This starter demonstrates the report grammar: a precise abstract, indexed sections, readable line length, restrained tables, explicit figures, and source-aware conclusions. Replace every sample sentence with evidence from the actual assignment.

01

Frame the decision before the detail

Begin with the decision, audience, evidence standard, and practical consequence. A report should make its reasoning inspectable. It should not force the reader to infer the purpose from a sequence of disconnected findings.

Use serif prose for the argument. Use sans-serif metadata for dates, status, sources, and operational annotations. This separation keeps the page calm while making the document easy to scan.

02

Use structure before decoration

Strong top rules mark major sections. Hairlines separate rows. Headings remain low weight and rely on scale. This provides enough hierarchy for dense technical documents without filling the page with boxes or background colors.

Every visual element must clarify hierarchy, relation, evidence, or action.

03

Keep comparison direct and legible

Tables use a strong cap, quiet row separators, uppercase headers, and right-aligned numeric columns. Avoid vertical rules unless they convey a real grouping boundary.

04

End with the consequence

A conclusion is not a repeated summary. State what the evidence changes, what remains uncertain, and which action follows. Separate fact, interpretation, and recommendation when the distinction matters.

Keep citations close to claims. Place full references in an appendix when the document requires them, but do not hide the source of a key number from the page where it appears.
